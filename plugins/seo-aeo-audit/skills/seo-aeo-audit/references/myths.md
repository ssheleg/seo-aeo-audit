# Myth guard — claims that do not survive 2026 evidence

Say this plainly when asked, offer the nearest thing that works, move on.

| Claim | Reality | What to do instead |
|---|---|---|
| "`llms.txt` improves AI visibility" | Google states it has no effect on Search (neither positive nor negative). In a 100M-site experiment it attracted 0.1% of AI-bot traffic and performed 3× worse than normal pages; in a 54-study meta-analysis it scores 2.0/10 (no evidence). Adoption has since risen 8.8× in a year and **97% of the published files still receive zero AI requests** — the file is being written and not read, which is the whole case in one number (benchmarks.md). Chrome's Lighthouse *does* check for it under agentic-browsing audits — a different surface, and optional | Keep the HTML clean and fetchable. Publish `llms.txt` only if you are targeting agentic browsers, and never bill it as a ranking or citation lever |
| "Serve Markdown mirrors of your pages for LLMs" | Markdown mirrors received **0%** AI-crawler visits versus 4.6% for the HTML versions; ChatGPT Deep Research does not follow `.md` links. One single-site test reported AIO gains — the evidence conflicts | Improve the HTML: front-loaded answer, plain-text facts, fewer links before the content. If you still want to test Markdown, do it as a measured experiment on one section |
| "Chunk your content for the retriever" | Chunk length, overlap and boundaries are chosen by the engine, not the publisher; Google says chunking is unnecessary. What does travel is one clear claim per passage | Write self-contained passages that each make one point |
| "Rewrite the text specifically for AI" | The one controlled academic benchmark found gains from citing authoritative sources, quoting experts, adding statistics and improving readability — keyword-style manipulation scored below baseline | Add evidence and clarity; that serves both audiences |
| "More schema = more AI citations" (canonical stance: schema is an **eligibility and entity aid on specific surfaces**, never a ranking or citation lever — mark up what is real and required, then stop; see aeo-geo.md F6, entity-and-brand.md G3, onpage-checks.md D1) | Google calls excessive Schema.org useless. Beyond that the evidence splits between **two named studies that disagree**: the 100M-site GEO experiment saw schema lift Google AIO while **reducing** citations in ChatGPT, Gemini and Copilot; the 10-week 29-domain LocalBusiness test found **no** gain in Google/Bing/Yahoo (Maps got worse) and AIO/AI Mode/Gemini/Grok at coin-flip significance, but a **positive** ChatGPT arm (+3.33 recommendation positions, +10pp citations, 91–93% significance — below the 95% bar). So the ChatGPT direction *and* the AIO direction are both **HYPOTHESIS**; neither result licenses volume. Figures and samples: benchmarks.md, "Contested metrics" | Mark up what is real and required for the features you actually want (products, jobs, events, breadcrumbs); stop there. If a client wants the schema question settled for their site, it is an experiment split by engine (experiments.md), not a rollout |
| "Add FAQPage markup for rich results" | Google discontinued FAQPage/Question/Answer rich results entirely | Answer the questions in the page body; use FAQ structure for users and extractability, not for a SERP feature |
| "AMP gives a ranking advantage" | The AMP cache is off, Search redirects to the publisher's AMP host, and AMP ranks like any other page | Optimize the normal page |
| "Update the publish date to refresh the page" | Google fingerprints pages and diffs them: title changes, substantial new sections and link changes count; date/`lastmod` bumps, JS, ads and boilerplate do not. Freshness is also relative to the SERP's age distribution, not the calendar | Do a substantial update, triggered by the age-versus-SERP check |
| "Publish more pages to grow" | The index is capacity-bound and quality-gated; adding pages dilutes and competes for finite slots. Fewer, better-indexed pages routinely win | Index tiering, consolidation, information gain |
| "Disavow anything a toxicity tool flags" | Penguin 4.0 devalues rather than demotes; third-party toxicity scores collapse context into one number and cause self-inflicted losses | Disavow only for a manual action, a dated negative-SEO spike with ranking loss, a known bought-link blast, or inherited spam |
| "`Disallow` the UTM URLs to protect crawl budget" | Canonicals already consolidate tracking variants — that is the case they were built for — and *crawled, not indexed* is the tag working, not a leak. A block cuts off a crawl Google is performing legitimately, cannot improve consolidation (a blocked URL never sees the canonical) and cannot touch the one real failure case: a parameterized URL that out-signals its canonical is chosen despite the tag, and `robots.txt` changes no signal. On hosted commerce the platform's own duplicates — Shopify's `/collections/*/products/*` paths and `?variant=` — outnumber tracking URLs before a single UTM exists | Confirm the scale first (GSC Pages report by reason, plus logs or Crawl Stats), fix the platform paths, then work the source: strip tracking parameters from internal links and from affiliate and partner placements you control, and leave genuine third-party tracking links crawlable — they are real referrals carrying real equity (technical-checks.md A2, play L13) |
| "Track one AI-visibility score" | Only ~2.4% of URLs are cited by ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google AIO simultaneously; the same brand's visibility differs by engine, persona and prompt format | Measure per engine and persona: mention, recommendation, then citation |
| "Publish a 'best [category]' listicle with yourself at #1" | When the model used a brand's own self-promotional article as its source, the brand was excluded from the recommendation in **69%** of cases; Google appears to have decoupled who is cited from who is recommended, and organic visibility of sites leaning on the tactic fell from early 2026 | Earn third-party placements; publish comparisons where you would be a natural pick if a stranger had written them |
| "Scale AI content for coverage" | Documented penalty profile: a directory of 850k AI articles took a "Scaled content abuse" action and lost both Google visibility and ChatGPT citations; 800+ generated pages preceded a 50k→10k monthly-visit collapse elsewhere; pure AI content ranks briefly then falls | Human-edited AI beats pure generation ~5×; ship fewer pages with original data |
| "Getting cited is the goal" | Users click a link inside an AI summary in roughly 1% of sessions, and a citation can point elsewhere while the answer names a competitor. That ~1% is the in-answer link specifically — it is not the same population as the 80% who still click a traditional result with an AIO present, or the 72%/90% vendor B2B figures; benchmarks.md, "Click economics", keeps the four measures apart | Optimize for being **named and recommended**, and measure business outcomes (calls, signups, bookings) |
| "Rank #1 in Google and AI visibility follows" | Rank is the strongest single correlate, but classic signals explain only 15–20% of ChatGPT recommendation variance, and a Bing-only site earned 90k+ ChatGPT citations. **The opposite observation is equally documented**: sites that lost 85% of their Google traffic lost ~75% of their ChatGPT traffic, and blocking Googlebot cuts ChatGPT referrals proportionally. Both are FIELD single cases pointing opposite ways, so the dependency claim is HYPOTHESIS (aeo-geo.md F4) | Treat AI visibility as a portfolio: rank somewhere, be extractable, be corroborated. **Do not read this row as "blocking Googlebot is AI-neutral"** — the measured effect of losing Google is a proportional loss of ChatGPT, and Googlebot cannot be split by purpose (technical-checks.md A1). Rank in at least one major engine and never bet a plan on a single index path |
| "Blocked-but-indexed URLs are diluting my site" | Google indexes the URL string without processing content — those pages cannot trigger duplicate filters or dilute quality. **This covers robots-*blocked* URLs only.** A mass of thin but *crawlable* pages is the opposite case: the content is processed, so it does count against host quality and does burn crawl budget (technical-checks.md A2, crawl-budget killers #4). Never merge the two into "low-value URLs are harmless" | Leave the block in place; only act if they steal impressions from a canonical page. For crawlable thin pages, run index tiering instead (technical-checks.md A2) |
| "A crawler hit means the model learned us" | Crawled → archived → extracted → filtered → deduplicated → mixed → trained. Content drops out at every stage, and deployed weights are frozen until retraining. **This is about the training stage.** It does not contradict aeo-geo.md F2, where URL accessibility scores 9.5/10 as a citation factor — that is the *retrieval* stage, where a live fetch at grounding time is a hard precondition for being quoted. Fetchable ⇒ can be cited today; fetched ⇏ known by the model | Measure retrieval and citation, not crawler logs, for AI *knowledge*; keep the URL fetchable anyway, because retrieval fails closed |
| "Optimize for `unicornClicks`" | It marks child accounts (COPPA/GDPR-K isolation), not premium users | Ignore it |
| "There are 200 ranking factors" | No such list exists; the number traces to one 2009 conference remark. Modern ranking runs hundreds-to-thousands of features plus ML overlays, and weighting is query-dependent (for scale, the Yandex leak showed ~690 factors) | Diagnose per query class on this site: what does this SERP reward, and what is this page missing (ranking-model.md) |
| "E-E-A-T is a ranking factor — raise our score" | E-E-A-T comes from the rater guidelines; raters evaluate, they do not rank. There is no E-E-A-T dial | Treat it as the specification of quality: demonstrated first-hand experience, named authors with evidence, transparent method — strongest requirement in YMYL |
| "AI wrote it, we just need to publish it" | Unedited AI content fails trust checks, readers increasingly detect it, and pure generation was beaten ~5× by AI-assisted human editing in controlled tests; scaled generation is a documented penalty profile | Keep AI for research, structure and analysis; keep the judgement, evidence and voice human, and verify every statistic |
| "Last-click attribution tells us what organic did" | The journey now spans AI answers, social, desktop research and phone calls; whole channels (calls, offline, AI referrals) sit outside the model, and one business found 80–90% of a channel's conversions arrived by phone | Multi-touch plus call tracking and offline conversion import; report branded search and direct as outcome metrics (demand-and-conversion.md) |
| "Google wants to see it there" | If the only justification for an element is what an engine supposedly wants, it is the wrong decision — pages that hold up are built from user expectation, and made-for-search-engines content is exactly what the helpful-content systems demote | Write down what a user expects on this page type, ship that, then check crawlability |
| "This quarter's framework is the thing to chase" | The industry produces a new buzzword every quarter; the brands winning in 2026 built authority before the panic and executed one strategy for years | Separate a fundamental shift from temporary noise — not every new tactic deserves a strategy overhaul |
| "SEO can fix AEO on its own" | The work spans brand, PR, product and review platforms; models pick names from memory and then look for support | Split the ownership explicitly in the plan |
| "Core Web Vitals decide whether AI cites us" | The fetchers that build AI answers read raw HTML: ChatGPT's does not load CSS or JS and has no access to CrUX. The unusually low layout shift measured across ChatGPT-cited pages is inherited from Google's index selection, not a preference of the bot (`FIELD`). What does travel is extraction cost — bloated HTML, JS-gated facts and slow responses get skipped | Fix what the fetcher actually sees: server response time, the facts in plain HTML, the answer front-loaded (aeo-geo.md F3). Keep CWV work for users and for Search tiebreaking (experience-signals.md) — do not bill it as an AI-citation lever |
| "Our two pages compete, so they split the link equity" | Nothing is split. Google runs **rotational testing** between competing URLs — alternating them in the SERP to see which resolves the intent — so both bounce in position and the pair loses combined clicks | Confirm with the GSC query→page breakdown plus a SERP-overlap test (>70% top-10 overlap = one intent), pick the winner on position/clicks/links/rework cost, 301 the losers and rewrite the internal links (intent-and-content.md) |
| "An indexing service will get our backlinks indexed" | Indexers can force a **crawl** only; indexing is a quality gate applied before inclusion, so "100% guaranteed" is false by construction. Published success rates are inflated by feeding the service URLs that are already indexed — in a field test of ten services on cohorts verified absent from the index, manual GSC submission landed about half, and one spam-network service ~11% (`FIELD`) | One internal link from a page Googlebot already crawls daily beats most paid tools. For your own URLs use the URL Inspection API / IndexNow, and fix the quality reason behind "Crawled – currently not indexed" (technical-checks.md) |
| "Every AI prompt is unique, so tracking AI visibility is pointless" | Across 1,754 prompts and 37,804 answers on five engines, same-intent prompts return the same brands at the same rate; 88–92% of real prompt pairs exceed 0.50 cosine similarity, and brand visibility is stable above ~0.50–0.60. What moves the answer is prompt **format**, not wording — lists/comparisons/tables surface ~20% more brands, keyword-explicit prompts ~25%; length and filler words do nothing (`STUDY`) | Track a fixed set on a 25/50/25 TOFU/MOFU/BOFU split, tag every prompt by format so a list is never compared against an open question, report per engine, and treat a changed qualifier (location, product, demographic, brand) as a new intent regardless of similarity score (aeo-geo.md F5) |
| "Google gives JavaScript about five seconds to render" | The Web Rendering Service runs on a **virtual clock** it controls, and pauses it while a resource request is in flight — so wall-clock waiting does not spend the budget. A test page whose two server-delayed API calls took 6–12 seconds rendered and indexed, and both calls reported back as 0.02s. Rendering ends when the event loop goes idle, not when a timer expires. The real ceiling is a page that never idles: a page fetching in a loop was cut off after 16–18 chained calls (48–54s) in live testing and after ~10 (~30s) for indexing, with the exact point drifting (`STUDY`, published method and test pages, 2026-07-23) | Stop budgeting render work against a stopwatch. Check that content does not depend on a chain that never settles, then verify what actually rendered — the stored render in *View crawled page* against the raw source (technical-checks.md A1) |
| "Multiple H1s hurt rankings" | Google states that one H1 and several both work for Search, with no penalty attached to the count. The one-H1 rule is an HTML/accessibility convention: HTML5's sectioned outline was never implemented by browsers, which is why the convention survived. Two responsive H1s are not cloaking while both carry the same meaning, and Google evaluates the mobile render, so the mobile H1 is the one with weight | Keep the count tidy for screen readers, not for rankings. Audit the **meaning**: if the mobile variant drops a subject-bearing word, that is the real loss. Prefer one H1 with the desktop-only tail inside a `span` over two H1s swapped by display classes (onpage-checks.md) |

## How to say it

Short, factual, no lecture, then the alternative:

> `llms.txt` will not change Google rankings or AI citations — Google documents
> that it is ignored, and in the largest published test the files drew 0.1% of
> AI-bot traffic. What did move citations in the same test was clean HTML,
> earned distribution and human-edited content. I have put those in the plan; if
> you want `llms.txt` for agentic browsers we can ship it as a five-minute
> extra, measured separately.

When the myth is about *measurement* rather than a tactic, name the substitute
metric in the same breath — otherwise the objection comes back as "so we can't
measure it at all":

> Prompts are not infinite in practice: in a 37,804-answer study, prompts with
> the same intent returned the same brands at the same rate, and what changed the
> answer was the prompt's format, not its wording. So we will not chase every
> phrasing. We track a fixed set — a quarter top-of-funnel, half middle, a
> quarter bottom — tagged by format, reported per engine, and we re-baseline when
> a qualifier changes. That is a number you can hold me to.
